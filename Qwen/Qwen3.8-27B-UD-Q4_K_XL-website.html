What it does

A dial that thinks like a tea master.

No screens in your pocket, no subscription, nothing to charge every night. Just a ring, a leaf, and the right moment.

01

The Glance Ring

A slow-turning amber ring shows exactly how much steep time is left, readable from across the kitchen — no tapping a phone to check on your pot.

02

Twelve Leaf Presets

From sencha to lapsang, Kessel remembers each leaf's ideal steep and water temperature. Turn the dial; it handles the math.

03

A Chime, Not an Alarm

When time is up you get two soft notes instead of a shriek — about the volume of a kettle sighing. Long-press to mute for late-night pots.

Good question — it doesn't guess. Brewing is just time and temperature, so Kessel counts down the exact steep for your selected leaf against a preset table (or your own). When the ring closes, the leaves are done.

Yes. Press and hold the dial to add a custom leaf — name, steep time, and target temperature — up to forty of them. Your grandmother's "just over two minutes" can finally be exact.

Kessel runs on a single CR2450 coin cell for roughly eighteen months, with an optional USB-C cradle. There is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or account — that's deliberate. A tea timer should never ask you to update its firmware.

No. The end-of-steep sound is a two-note chime at roughly 65 dB — quieter than most doorbells and softer than your kettle's whistle. In silent mode the ring simply turns pale instead, so you still get the signal without a single note.
